Two accounts, one rule

Savings account — no debit card on it

Tithe, emergency fund and the jars live here. None of this money is needed this fortnight, so it shouldn’t be one tap away. If there’s no card attached, you can’t spend it by accident.

Everyday account — card on this one

Rent, food, Myki, subscriptions, repayments and your own spending money. One rule: if it’s in this account, you can spend it. No tracking, no guilt, no asking.

The jars

These are the costs that don’t arrive every fortnight — birthdays, a flight, car rego. They’re the ones that feel like surprises, and they’re the reason people splash when pay is good and panic when it isn’t. Put a little aside each time and they stop being surprises. Change any number to suit.

That’s what should be sitting in the savings account by now. If the real balance is lower, something has been dipped into — which is worth knowing, not worth panicking about.

Before you buy a car

Turn the car jar on before you buy, not after. Rego and TAC, comprehensive insurance for a driver under 25, a service, tyres — it adds up to somewhere around $3,000–4,000 a year before you put a drop of petrol in. That’s roughly $120–150 a fortnight, every fortnight, forever.

Set the jar to that number and watch what happens to the bottom of the list. Then decide. Get an insurance quote first — for a young driver it’s the biggest and most unpredictable piece, and it’s worth knowing the real figure before you fall in love with a car.

Worth knowing

The rent help moves both ways

$140 a fortnight is the normal setting. When you’re earning well, tell me and I’ll drop it. When a fortnight goes wrong, tell me and I’ll put more in. Work out the number here first so you know what you’re asking for. Asking isn’t failing; it’s the arrangement working.

Tax coming out is normal

Tax is worked out as if every fortnight looked like that one, so a big fortnight pulls more out. If your year total stays low you get the extra back when you lodge. Lodge a return every single year, even if you think you earned nothing.

Rent, food, insurance

The three where missing a payment has consequences you can’t talk your way out of. If a fortnight is short, keep these, let the repayments wait, and tell me. That’s information, not failure.
